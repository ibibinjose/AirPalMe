# Marketing Psychology Skill

This skill applies psychological principles, cognitive heuristics, and behavioral economics to marketing, copy, product onboarding, pricing architecture, and conversion rate optimization (CRO).

---

## Strategic Thinking

- **Jobs to Be Done** — Frame around customer outcomes, not features. A drill buyer wants a hole.
- **First Principles** — Ask why each tactic exists before copying competitors.
- **Inversion** — "What guarantees failure?" then prevent each failure mode.
- **Pareto (80/20)** — 20% of channels/customers drive 80% of results. Cut the rest.
- **Theory of Constraints** — Fix the system bottleneck first. Traffic low? Don't optimize conversion yet.
- **Second-Order Thinking** — Flash sales boost revenue but train customers to wait for discounts.
- **Occam's Razor** — Check the obvious first (broken form, page speed) before complex hypotheses.

---

## Buyer Psychology

- **Fundamental Attribution Error** — Blame process, not people. Conversion drops usually mean confusing checkout, not lazy users.
- **Social Proof** — Show counts, testimonials, logos, reviews, trending indicators.
- **Mimetic Desire** — Waitlists, exclusivity, and visible demand trigger desire.
- **Mere Exposure** — Consistent brand presence builds preference over time.
- **Availability Heuristic** — Case studies make success feel achievable.
- **Confirmation Bias** — Align messaging with existing beliefs; fighting them fails.
- **Endowment Effect** — Free trials create ownership; users reluctant to leave.
- **IKEA Effect** — Customization and setup wizards increase commitment.
- **Zero-Price Effect** — Free is psychologically different from cheap. $0 > $1 jump is larger than $2 > $1.
- **Present Bias** — "Start saving today" beats "ROI in 6 months."
- **Status-Quo Bias** — Reduce friction to switch: "Import data in one click."
- **Default Effect** — Pre-select the preferred plan; opt-out beats opt-in.
- **Paradox of Choice** — Three pricing tiers beat seven. Recommend one "best for most."
- **Goal-Gradient Effect** — Progress bars and "almost there" messaging drive completion.
- **Peak-End Rule** — Design memorable peaks (surprise upgrades) and strong endings (thank you pages).
- **Zeigarnik Effect** — "You're 80% done" creates pull. Incomplete profiles leverage this.
- **Mental Accounting** — "$3/day" feels different than "$90/month." "Less than your morning coffee" reframes expense.
- **Regret Aversion** — Money-back guarantees and "no commitment" messaging reduce fear.
- **Pratfall Effect** — Admitting a small weakness builds trust: "Not the cheapest, but..."
- **Curse of Knowledge** — Test copy with newcomers; your product seems obvious to you but confuses them.

## Persuasion Techniques

- **Reciprocity** — Give value first (free content, tools, generous tiers). People feel obligated to return favors.
- **Commitment & Consistency** — Get small commitments first (email → trial → paid). Each step increases next likelihood.
- **Authority** — Expert endorsements, certifications, "featured in" logos.
- **Scarcity / Urgency** — Limited-time offers, stock warnings, exclusive access. Use only when genuine.
- **Loss Aversion** — Losses feel 2x more painful than gains. Frame as "Don't miss out," not "You could gain."
- **Anchoring** — Show higher price first (competitor, enterprise tier) to set expectations.
- **Decoy Effect** — Add inferior tier to make preferred tier look obviously better.
- **Framing** — "90% success" vs "10% failure" are identical but feel different. Frame positively.
- **Contrast** — Show "before" clearly; contrast makes "after" vivid.
- **Foot-in-the-Door** — Start small, escalate: free → paid → annual → enterprise.
- **Door-in-the-Face** — Start with enterprise price, reveal affordable starter. Contrast feels like a deal.

## Pricing Psychology

- **Charm Pricing** — $99 feels cheaper than $100. Left digit dominates. Use .99 for value positioning.
- **Round Pricing** — Signals premium ($500) vs value ($497). Choose based on positioning.
- **Rule of 100** — Under $100: percentage discounts seem larger. Over $100: absolute discounts seem larger.
- **Good-Better-Best** — Three tiers; middle is target. Expensive tier = looks reasonable. Cheap tier = anchor.
- **Price Reframing** — "$1/day" < "$30/month." Reframe into familiar mental accounts.

## Design & Delivery

- **Hick's Law** — More choices = slower decisions = more abandonment. One clear CTA beats three.
- **AIDA** — Attention → Interest → Desire → Action. Move sequentially through each stage.
- **BJ Fogg** — Behavior = Motivation × Ability × Prompt. All three required.
- **EAST** — Easy (reduce friction), Attractive (appeal), Social (show others), Timely (right moment).
- **Activation Energy** — Pre-fill forms, templates, quick wins. Make first step trivially easy.
- **Nudge Theory** — Defaults, ordering, friction guide behavior without restricting choice.
- **Cobra Effect** — Incentives backfire. Referral bonus might attract low-quality referrals gaming system. Test carefully.

---

## Growth & Retention

- **Network Effects** — Shared workspaces, integrations, communities increase value with scale.
- **Switching Costs** — Build ethically: integrations, data accumulation, customization, team adoption.
- **Flywheel** — Content → traffic → leads → customers → case studies → content. Each powers next.
- **Feedback Loops** — More users → better discovery → more users. Identify and strengthen loops.
- **Compounding** — Small consistent gains compound over time. Content, SEO, brand build slowly.
